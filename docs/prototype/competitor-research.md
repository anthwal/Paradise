### What builds trust
A licence number, ABN, real local project photos, verifiable reviews (with a link to the source), a physical or workshop location, a phone number, named people, and clear finish and material information.

### What causes hesitation
A missing licence number, stock imagery, long forms, a form as the only contact route, slow responses, unverifiable testimonials, and vague compliance language.

### What should be visible immediately
What the business does, real examples of the work, a tap-to-call phone, a quote button, the service area and the licence number.

### How customers compare businesses
Photos of similar jobs, reviews and ratings, recommendations, licence and insurance, response speed and indicative price.

### What makes quoting feel easy
5–7 fields, optional photo upload, a choice of call, email or form, and a clear statement of what happens next.

### Segment needs

| Segment | What they look for before enquiring |
|---|---|
| Homeowners renovating | Inspiration by product type, colour and finish options, examples similar to their house, reassurance about the process |
| New-home builders (owner-occupiers) | Coordinated frontage design, timing relative to the build program, estate design guidelines |
| Architects and designers | Evidence of custom and bespoke capability, finishes, ability to work from drawings |
| Builders and contractors | Licence and insurance, reliability, multi-dwelling experience, documentation |
| Commercial property owners | Façade and screen experience, minimal disruption, compliance paperwork |
| Developers | Multi-unit consistency, staged delivery, one point of contact |

### Australian English and terminology used
"Enquiry", "quote" (not estimate), "colour", "aluminium", "metre", "licence" (noun) and "licensed" (adjective), "balustrade" (not railing), "pool fencing", "privacy screen", "suburb", "mobile", "body corporate", "powder-coated".

### Strengths, weaknesses and opportunities (from available evidence)

| Competitor | Strength | Weakness (as observable) | Opportunity for Paradise |
|---|---|---|---|
| Schmidt | Clear gate specialism, in-house manufacture, volume claim | Limited independent reviews found | Lead with project storytelling and evidence rather than claims |
| A Grade Aluminium | Long track record, broad catalogue | Broad list, little differentiation in listings | Curate services by customer goal, not product catalogue |
| Kelmac | Explicit service area | Generic product listing | State the service area clearly once confirmed |
| Next Generation | Factory and years stated | Conflicting addresses across directories | Consistent business details everywhere (Paradise has the same problem, so fix it) |
| Absolute Fencing | Heritage and local-made message | Aluminium-fencing focus only | Show the breadth: gates, screens, glass, balustrades, commercial |
| Steel and Glass Co | Experience claim | Generic "20 years" claim | Show the work instead of asserting it |
| Garmisch | Strong search rankings, longevity | Heritage-led rather than design-led | A contemporary architectural presentation |
| GC Aluminium Gates & Fences | Same mix, same suburb | Listing-only presence found | Out-present it with a premium portfolio site |
| Total Vision | Licence and guarantee up front | Pool-specialist only | Show the licence prominently (once supplied) across a wider range |
| Oxworks | States a lead time, which reduces uncertainty | Product-led, more supplier than installer | Explain the process and what happens next |
