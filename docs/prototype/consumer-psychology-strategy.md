# Consumer Psychology & Persuasion Strategy

Principles from the reference literature, translated into **specific, ethical website decisions**. No manipulative tactics are used: no false urgency, countdown timers, fabricated scarcity, pop-ups or invented social proof. Book concepts are summarised in our own words, not quoted.

## 1. The customer's story (StoryBrand: Donald Miller)
- **The hero is the property owner, not the company.** They want a property that looks finished and feels private and safe.
- **Problem:** an unfinished frontage, a pool that needs fencing, a balcony that needs a balustrade, no privacy. Underneath that is the worry of choosing a tradie who won't deliver a result that looks right.
- **Guide:** Paradise shows empathy ("openings, slopes and pier spacing are rarely standard") and authority (real projects).
- **Plan:** a simple 4-step process (enquire → measure and design → fabricate → install).
- **Call to action:** one primary action everywhere, "Request a quote". The transitional action is "View our work".
- **Success:** "The metalwork that finishes the house."
→ Home hero copy, the process section, and consistent CTAs.

## 2. Positioning (Obviously Awesome: April Dunford; Positioning: Ries & Trout)
- **Competitive alternatives:** catalogue-style fencing companies, off-the-shelf DIY panels.
- **Unique attributes (evidenced):** made to measure, coordinated frontages, glass and aluminium, residential through to commercial.
- **Category frame:** "fabrication with an eye for design". This sits between commodity fencing and an architectural studio.
- **Owning one idea in the customer's mind:** *the whole frontage, designed as one.*
→ Home "Whole frontages, not just a gate." and the Custom Fabrication page.

## 3. Influence (Robert Cialdini)

| Principle | Ethical application | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Authority | Expertise is shown through design guidance ("Design considerations") and real projects, not titles | Service pages, project stories |
| Social proof | Real photos now. Verified reviews only, with permission, linked to Facebook. Slots are clearly marked until supplied. | Reviews section, Projects |
| Consistency | One visual language, one CTA wording, consistent finishes. Small "micro-commitments" such as browsing a filter or the chooser lead naturally to the form. | Site-wide, services chooser |
| Liking | Plain, warm Australian English. Human "we" voice. Approachable process copy. | All copy |
| Reciprocity | Useful advice before asking for anything: design considerations, horizontal vs vertical, glass vs aluminium | Service pages, FAQ |
| Scarcity | **Not used.** No capacity data supports it. | — |

## 4. Making it stick (Made to Stick: Chip & Dan Heath)
- **Simple:** one idea per section.
- **Concrete:** "Panels sit square and gaps stay even from end to end." This is tangible, not "quality workmanship".
- **Credible:** testable credibility through photos the visitor can inspect in the lightbox.
- **Story:** project stories with scope, style and finish.

## 5. Decision-making (Thinking, Fast and Slow: Daniel Kahneman; The Choice Factory: Richard Shotton)
- **System 1 first impression:** within seconds the visitor sees real metalwork (the hero strips), a plain headline and one obvious button. This is the "what, why, how" test.
- **Cognitive ease:** a calm palette, generous space, five navigation items, short sentences and an uncluttered form.
- **Choice architecture:** the goal-based chooser reduces choice overload. Contact links from service pages pre-select the service.
- **Framing:** "Request a quote" frames the action as low-commitment. "What happens next" removes uncertainty, which reduces loss aversion.
- **Pratfall / honesty effect (Shotton):** honest placeholders ("To confirm") and no inflated claims build credibility with a sceptical audience.
- **Peak-end rule:** the form ends on a clear, reassuring confirmation (demo modal, with a summary of what was sent).

## 6. Advertising craft (Ogilvy on Advertising)
- Specific, factual copy outsells adjectives, so the prototype avoids "best", "leading" and "unmatched".
- Photographs of the actual product in use are the most persuasive visual, which is why the client's photos lead.
- Headlines carry the promise. Captions are read, so every photo is captioned descriptively.

## 7. This Is Marketing (Seth Godin) and Blue Ocean Strategy (Kim & Mauborgne)
- **The smallest viable audience:** owners of contemporary homes and builders who care how the frontage looks. Do not try to please everyone.
- **Blue ocean move:** compete on *design coordination and presentation*, which competitors do not emphasise, rather than on price or years.
- **$100M Offers (Hormozi), applied lightly:** increase perceived likelihood of success (photos, process) and reduce perceived effort (photo upload, short form). Do not fabricate guarantees or bonuses.

## 8. Reputation, perception and consistency (Machiavelli, *The Prince*; Robert Greene)
Translated into ethical business communication:
- **Reputation is built on what people can see and verify.** Let the work speak. Avoid claims the business cannot back up, because one exposed exaggeration undoes the rest.
- **Consistency of appearance signals reliability.** Identical business details everywhere (fix the conflicting addresses), a consistent visual identity and consistent CTAs.
- **Confidence without arrogance:** say less, show more. Restrained typography and whitespace read as assured (*The Laws of Human Nature*: people judge character from small, consistent signals).
- **Long-term relationships over the single sale:** a clear process, honest timelines once confirmed, and builder and commercial pathways.

## 9. Applied UX checklist

| Goal | Implementation |
|---|---|
| First impression in about 5 seconds | Hero: what (headline and sub-headline), proof (photos), how (Request a quote) |
| Visual proof | 45 real photos, featured stories, lightbox for close inspection |
| Reduced cognitive load | 5 top-level nav items, mega menu with thumbnails, one primary CTA |
| Risk reduction | Process, what happens next, contact details, licence placeholder, verified reviews only |
| Choice architecture | Goal-based chooser, service pre-selection, gallery filters |
| Authority through presentation | Typographic system, consistent grid, editorial layouts |
| Emotional connection | "The metalwork that finishes the house", before-you-start design guidance |
| Conversion | Sticky mobile Call / Quote bar, CTAs at the end of every page, no pop-ups or timers |
